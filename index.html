The problem

                
One instructor. Twelve terminals.
No idea who's drowning.

                
In a hands-on lab, the students who ask for help are rarely the ones who need it most. The struggling ones go quiet — retrying the same command, staring at the same error — and you find out after the session ends, when the window to intervene has already closed.

How it works

                
From silent struggle to timely help, in three steps

            

            

                

                    
1

                    
The agent watches the work

                    
A lightweight desktop agent reads the signal in the work itself: commands run, errors hit, time gone idle. No screen recordings to scrub through — just live activity from every lab machine.

                    

                        terminal commands
                        error output
                        idle time
                    

                

                

                    
2

                    
Stuck detection fires

                    
Repeated failures. A long idle right after an error. Circling the same dead end. The moment a student crosses the stuck threshold, they're flagged — in real time, not in the post-lab report.

The payoff

                
Every lab hour works harder

                
Catching the stuck moment isn't just about rescuing one student. It changes the economics of the whole session.

            

            

                

                    

                        
                    

                    
Practice time stays on target

                    
A student who burns 40 minutes on a broken config or a mistyped path isn't practising the skill the session was built around. Adaptly catches the detour early and steers the time back to what the lab was designed to teach.

                    
                    
→ more of every session spent on the intended skills

                

                

                    

                        
                    

                    
Instructors stop firefighting

                    
The “what does this error mean?” queue is handled by graduated hints before it ever reaches a human. Instructors spend the session on judgment calls and teaching — the work that actually needs their expertise.

                    

                        
student_04stuck on file permissionsAI · resolved · hint L1

                        
student_16repeat error · nmap syntaxAI · resolved · hint L2

                        
student_02conceptual gap · sudo policyescalated → instructor

                    

                    
→ troubleshooting load shifts from instructor to platform

                

                

                    

                        
                    

                    
Scale without diluting quality

                    
When detection and first-line help are automatic, one instructor can genuinely support a larger cohort — and each student gets faster, more consistent coverage than walking the room ever delivered.

→ bigger cohorts, better coverage per student

                

            

            

                
More students per instructor. More practice per hour. Better training — not just cheaper training.
